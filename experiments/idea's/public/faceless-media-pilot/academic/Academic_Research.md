## Abstract
This proposal develops a method to evaluate whether an independent content creator can use AI-assisted scripting, voice and visual tools to produce original, factual, rights-cleared educational videos at manageable cost. The idea was recovered from October 2026 discussions seeking an evidence-based faceless media venture, including niche selection, tool evaluations, expenditure tracking, production failures and audience measurements. A previous conversation summary describes a drafted video, but no primary performance data were independently audited. The proposed developmental mixed-methods pilot measures creation minutes, cash cost, factual and media-rights compliance, repeatability, upload acceptance and normalized engagement indicators across two niches. Prospective data collection and review must precede empirical conclusions. The contribution is a replicable research protocol and documentation format, not evidence that the approach is already profitable.

# CHAPTER I - THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND
## Introduction
Independent creators can use generative tools for ideas, scripts, audiovisual content and editing assistance. These efficiencies are uncertain because output must still be made accurate, original, accessible and lawful. OECD (2024) discusses creative-sector opportunities and intellectual-property tensions; it does not establish profitability for individual channels. This study therefore treats the human review and documentation burden as part of production cost.

## Background of the Study
The recovered owner proposal was a faceless multi-platform media experiment with public discussion of niche selection, costs, tool failures and monetization outcomes. A prior assistant proposed a production pipeline; that design is a later recommendation, not independently recovered evidence that it operates. A reported video draft in October 2026 does not establish posting, channel eligibility, viewership or research findings.

## Statement of the Problem
There is inadequate local project evidence to determine the effectiveness, quality or commercial feasibility of a hybrid human-AI faceless media workflow. The proposed study will investigate repeatability, editorial risk and costs while describing the uncertainty caused by small samples and platform policies.

## Objectives
**General:** design and evaluate a controlled AI-assisted faceless educational video production workflow.

**Specific:** (1) document the complete editorial process; (2) measure direct expenditure and labor per accepted video; (3) assess verifiable claims and content-rights traceability; (4) document upload success and required synthetic-media disclosures; (5) compare two candidate niches over equivalent observation windows; and (6) propose evidence-based expansion or termination rules.

## Research Questions
RQ1. What production time and cash outlay are observed for each accepted video? RQ2. What portion of verifiable claims has retained credible support? RQ3. What portion of used audiovisual assets has documented commercial or educational rights? RQ4. How consistent is upload success and content quality across repeated exports? RQ5. What differences in available platform retention metrics are observed between candidate niches?

## Hypotheses
H1 (unverified): AI assistance combined with editorial quality checks may reduce labor time relative to comparable manual production. H2 (unverified): documented editorial review may reduce preventable post-publication corrections. The proposed micro-sample cannot establish robust population-wide causality; quantitative results are primarily descriptive.

## Significance
Creators can obtain a reproducible accounting model; learners may benefit from better explained technical topics; educators can assess the integrity and accessibility of AI-assisted teaching materials; later researchers can replicate or extend the pilot.

## Scope and Delimitations
Candidate setting is one independent creator producing original English or bilingual educational explainers, with YouTube as the principal measurement environment and optional secondary platforms. The pilot proposes three videos for each of two predefined niches, selected before data collection. No sample survey or respondents currently exists. Platform distribution algorithms, topic complexity, personal skill, third-party rights and changing partner rules constrain generalization.

## Operational Definitions
Accepted video: output passing documented factual, rights, technical and disclosure gates. Source coverage: supported verifiable claims divided by all verifiable claims. Direct cost/video: attributable paid expenses divided by accepted outputs. Average viewing percentage: platform-reported measure, used only with a named platform and fixed observation date.

# CHAPTER III - RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
## Research Design
Use prospective developmental research with descriptive quantitative logs and structured qualitative observations. Optional reviewer evaluation may be included with consent. This proposal is not a randomized trial or completed system validation.

## Research Environment and Sampling
One independent editing workstation and published platform dashboards form the proposed setting. Choose two niches in advance based on transparent selection rules and develop three original videos in each niche; match approximate duration and content difficulty. External reviewers are not presumed recruited. A manual baseline, when technically comparable, may be tested separately and fully documented.

## Research Instruments
**Table 1 - Planned measures**
| Measure | Instrument | Computation or rule |
|---|---|---|
| Production time | Timestamped creator log | Minutes/accepted video |
| Direct cash cost | Expense and credit register | PHP/accepted video |
| Fact traceability | Claim-source checklist | Supported/total verifiable claims |
| Rights traceability | License/permission register | Cleared/total used assets |
| Corrections | Versioned incident log | Confirmed issues per video |
| Upload success | Platform export register | Accepted/attempted uploads |
| Viewer retention | Dated platform analytics | Platform-native metric and window |

## Data Collection Procedure
Record the approved protocol and content themes before editing. Capture manual and assisted task durations, per-asset rights decisions and claim sources. Obtain an independent content review when possible. Publish only after the documented quality gate. Save dashboard exports at consistent periods, such as 7 and 28 days. Retain zero-view and rejected exports to avoid survivor bias. Store only aggregated non-identifying audience analytics.

## Development and Evaluation
The process follows requirements -> scripted prototype -> iterative editorial review -> quality audit -> publish -> logged observation -> improvements. Apply the same checklist to each niche. Track missing data explicitly. Measure repeatability from saved masters rather than merely counting drafts.

## Statistical Treatment
Report counts, medians, ranges and interquartile ranges for small samples. Use numerator and denominator for every fraction. Cross-niche comparisons remain exploratory. Do not use invented p-values, causal claims or general population conclusions from six purposively selected videos. Additional sampling and a preregistered inferential plan would be needed for formal hypothesis testing.

## Ethics and Information Security
Require voluntary informed consent for human evaluators and institutional review when applicable. Avoid unnecessary personal-data collection. Protect rights-cleared media records, honor commercial licenses and disclose relevant realistic AI-generated visuals. Do not claim ethics approval without documentation.

## Proposed Analysis
Test whether each candidate niche generates enough original material, acceptable video quality and tolerable editing demands. Use preregistered minimum editorial standards and financial spending cap. Report negative outcomes, tool failures and missing observations. The expected value is reliable information for a stop-or-expand decision, **not an expected finding that AI is superior**.

# CHAPTER V - SUMMARY, CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
## Summary
The proposal structures a small creator-business experiment into an academic method with accountable original media, source and rights management, cost recording and consistent evaluation.

## Evidence-Bounded Conclusions
Existing public sources establish relevant AI risk and platform policy context. No recovered measured data establish audience growth, improved learning, operational savings or positive return for this specific project. The work is a **preliminary academic research proposal**, not a completed thesis.

## Recommendations
Run the pilot with preregistered quality thresholds, direct expense logging, full media-rights checks and preserved correction history. Verify application-time platform rules. Publish completed empirical results only after actual collection, review and ethical clearance when needed.

## Future Directions
Larger samples, multilingual content, cross-creator comparisons, accessibility and audience learning outcomes may justify further research after a valid pilot.
