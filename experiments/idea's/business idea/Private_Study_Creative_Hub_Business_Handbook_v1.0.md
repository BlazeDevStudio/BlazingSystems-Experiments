# THE PRIVATE STUDY & CREATIVE HUB
## A lean-launch business handbook for a student-focused Philippine workspace and café
**Concept edition 1.0 | 16 October 2026 | Philippines (Northern Mindanao pilot assumption)**

**Status:** business concept and operating plan, not a construction drawing, guaranteed quotation, or legal/tax opinion.

> **Business principle:** Give people freedom to create and study in their own space while protecting everyone else's quiet, comfort, privacy, and safety.

---

# PREFACE - THE DECISION IN ONE PAGE

The proposed business is a student-oriented **private workspace and creative hub with a small public café**. It combines private study/project rooms with useful equipment, borrowable art materials, optional paid PCs and monitors, public café seating, a separately charged PisoWiFi connection, and metered café power outlets.

The mature vision is **8 solo rooms + 4 Duo+ rooms + 2 group rooms + café** (14 private rooms total). **Do not build all 14 rooms at launch.** The recommended low-risk first phase is **1 solo + 1 Duo+ + 1 group room, plus 2-3 café tables**, only in a suitable existing property with applicable permissions.

The investment thesis is simple: **keep fixed monthly expenses low enough to survive a slow opening**. A PHP 200,000 pilot is a **conditional spending target**, not a promise; it assumes a pre-existing space, substantial reused furniture, limited refurbishing, safe electrical and ventilation systems, and some owner-led labor. A rented full-sized facility with credible sound isolation could exceed PHP 1 million, depending on actual quotations.

**The owner will initially run the facility personally.** This is owner-operated, low-overhead work, NOT fully passive income. Rooms can be automated but arrivals, customer support, cleaning, incidents and food hygiene still require attention. Never leave occupied private rooms unstaffed just because payment is automated.

**Opening rule:** first prove willingness to pay using actual reservations; then expand in response to repeat demand. Do not sign a large commercial lease merely because the concept looks attractive on paper.

# CHAPTER 1 - PURPOSE, CUSTOMERS AND POSITIONING

## 1.1 Mission and promise
An affordable, respectful place for focused study, academic projects, art, design, programming, tutoring and group collaboration. It is neither a conventional computer shop nor just a coffee shop.

The **café** is the accessible public entry point. The **private rooms** are the premium service: privacy, quiet, standard charging, free Wi-Fi and a focused workspace.

## 1.2 Primary customers
- Senior-high students working on assignments and performances, with age-appropriate supervision and safety measures.
- College students working on capstones, drafts, reports, coding, presentations and research.
- Tutors, student organizations, freelance creators and exam-review partners, when their activities fit the same house rules.

## 1.3 Jobs customers hire the business to do
- "I need a place where I can concentrate alone."
- "Our group needs a table, a screen and a whiteboard without disturbing others."
- "We have a project to make but no convenient workspace."
- "We need affordable short-term space with useful equipment."
- "We only need drinks and a table right now."

## 1.4 What not to promise
Do not market rooms as acoustically *soundproof* unless independently tested. Do not promise total secrecy or emergency-proof privacy. Do not claim equipment is universally free if consumables or high-power use have limits. Do not imply minors may use hazardous tools unsupervised.

# CHAPTER 2 - COMPLETE BUSINESS FORMAT

## 2.1 Customer-facing service map
**ZONE A - PUBLIC CAFÉ:** open seating, food/drinks, table-based activities, paid PisoWiFi, paid charging stations. Normal conversational volume is permitted if it does not disturb neighboring rooms.

**ZONE B - PRIVATE ROOMS:** Solo (1 person), Duo+ (up to 3), Group (up to 6 per room, final capacity subject to building review). Standard outlets and room Wi-Fi included. Computer access, multi-monitor stations and advanced equipment are optional extras.

**ZONE C - STAFF AND UTILITIES:** cashier, drink storage, wash area, network cabinet, maintenance storage, cameras viewing permitted shared circulation spaces, first-aid and safety equipment.

**ZONE D - FUTURE WORKSHOP:** noisy, dusty, hot, sharp or fume-producing work. Only launch this zone if a safe, approved, equipped and supervised space is available; do NOT permit soldering, drilling or chemical work in an ordinary enclosed study room.

## 2.2 Phase comparison
| Item | Pilot version | Long-term target |
|---|---|---|
| Solo rooms | 1 | 8 |
| Duo+ rooms | 1 | 4 |
| Group rooms | 1 | 2 |
| Café | 2-3 tables | Size determined by demand |
| Employees | Owner-operated | Add staff when workload justifies |
| Build strategy | Adapt existing legal space | Phased modular construction |
| Business decision | Test paid demand | Expand only after proof |

## 2.3 Why full build must wait
Fourteen private rooms increase the total cost of walls, doors, ventilation, electrical outlets, lighting, furniture, cleaning, fire precautions and toilets. Quiet separation may become the most expensive promise. Empty rooms are not proof of poor product; they may indicate a site, marketing, price or school-calendar problem.

# CHAPTER 3 - PRICES, CAPS AND TIME RULES

All rates below are **proposed launch-test prices in PHP**, not verified local market quotations. Change them after surveying customers and measuring actual margins.

## 3.1 Private room rates
| Room | Standard price | Capacity | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Solo | PHP 49 / room / hour | 1 | One user, no companion |
| Duo+ | PHP 79 / room / hour | Up to 3 | Fixed rate, whether 1, 2 or 3 attend |
| Group | PHP 30 / person / hour | Max 6 | Minimum bill equal to 4 people |
| Group minimum | PHP 120 / room / hour | 1-4 charged as 4 | Prevents very low revenue on a large room |

Group examples per hour: 4 people PHP 120; 5 people PHP 150; 6 people PHP 180. A party of 2-3 pays the four-person minimum if it insists on renting a group room. Occupants cannot exceed the posted physical capacity even if willing to pay more.

## 3.2 Optional four-hour promotions
Solo PHP 169/4 hours; Duo+ PHP 279/4 hours; Group PHP 105/person/4 hours with a minimum total equivalent to four people. These are experiments and reduce realized revenue per room-hour. Never model discounted customers as if they pay full rates.

## 3.3 Check-in and overtime
Bookings are prepaid; show the start/end time and included guests. A person entering the room counts as an occupant. If someone arrives late, do not reset the time for the entire group. Proposed grace for ending: 5 minutes when no next booking, at operator discretion. Post clear refunds, reschedules, late arrival and damage procedures before first sale.

## 3.4 Add-ons to test
| Add-on | Indicative price | Operating note |
|---|---:|---|
| PC workstation | PHP 25-35/hour | Only after cost/payback analysis |
| Premium multi-monitor station | PHP 45-60/hour total | Include PC in bundle |
| Extra monitor | PHP 10-15/hour | Not mandatory for basic use |
| Ordinary creative supplies | Included in limited quantities | Keep inventory limits |
| Specialized equipment | Posted use fee or deposit | Training and supervision |
| Printing | Per-page | Set price against consumables |

Free supplies are consumables *within sensible limits*, not unrestricted take-home merchandise. Establish borrowing logs and replacement values for equipment.

# CHAPTER 4 - PUBLIC CAFÉ POLICY

## 4.1 Core idea
**ORDER ANYTHING. USE YOUR TABLE. TWO HOURS INCLUDED.**

- **Solo:** purchase any one available item; up to two hours of seating are included.
- **Two people:** each purchases any item; up to two hours are included.
- **Three or more as one party:** each person pays at least **PHP 60 upfront**, fully credited toward food and drinks. Two hours of seating included.
- **Additional time:** test PHP 20/person/additional hour, subject to seating availability. This is a seat-time charge, not a food credit.
- Café Wi-Fi is **not free**: customers buy access from the independent PisoWiFi system.
- Café electrical outlets are **not free**: customers may use clearly labelled, protected coin-operated charging stations.
- Takeaway customers need no seating minimum. Private-room customers purchasing food do not need to meet a café seating threshold unless they also occupy café seating.

Avoid the loophole where a party of six registers as three independent pairs at adjacent tables. Treat a group that deliberately occupies a shared table cluster as one party, consistently and without intrusive enforcement.

## 4.2 Belongings and virtual boundaries
Each café table has an assigned **personal-use footprint**, including suitable floor space alongside or beneath the table for bags and project materials. Customers may put belongings on the floor **inside** their zone. Study, sketch, cards, board games and clean creative activities are allowed **at the table**, not as floor-spreading activities. Nobody may spill into a neighbor's area or shared circulation space. Aisles, exits and accessible routes always stay clear. Use subtle floor corners, tile changes or table labels to mark zones.

## 4.3 Atmosphere and behavior
Normal conversation is permitted. Loud music, yelling, strong odors, disruptive activities, dangerous tools, cooking appliances, electrical tampering, and damaging wet art processes are not. Do not let customers use food tables for hazardous soldering or cutting work.

## 4.4 Time records and customer experience
Start the seating timer **when the order is served**. Put the expiry on the receipt or digital ticket. Offer a 10-minute grace period during non-busy hours. A new order does **not automatically restart** the clock. Allow optional extensions only when spare seating exists. Display prices and terms clearly before ordering.

# CHAPTER 5 - PAID INTERNET, POWER AND DEVICES

## 5.1 Two separated networks
**Private-room network:** included Wi-Fi with sensible fair-use limits; separate VLAN or SSID; customer-to-customer isolation where practical.

**Café network:** independent PisoWiFi portal, coin/voucher access, limited to public internet. Never allow the public network to reach the cashier, room booking controller, management ports or CCTV recorder.

Admin systems and cameras need a **third protected management network**. Back up configurations and keep logging proportional and privacy-conscious.

## 5.2 Café charging concept
Planning test: PHP 5/30 minutes, PHP 10/hour, PHP 15/two hours for normal phone/laptop charging. These are service prices, **not** electricity tariff claims.

Install a few coin-operated stations first. Provide clear load limits. A competent licensed electrical practitioner should check the design, protected circuits, breaker sizing, RCD/RCBO/GFCI arrangements as appropriate, outlet placement and installation. Keep outlets away from drink spills. Ban kettles, cooking heaters, soldering irons, extension daisy chains and unattended damaged chargers.

## 5.3 Private room policy
Standard safe outlets and Wi-Fi are included in room rent. High-power workshop devices, premium PCs and printing are separate services if offered. Keep critical network, payment and room-lock control separate so a PisoWiFi outage does not unlock rooms or erase paid time.

## 5.4 Privacy of device data
Do not inspect customer laptop content. For rented PCs use guest profiles, account sign-out, privacy partitions, security updates and session cleaning. Never advertise a PC as private if previous customers' files or passwords can persist.

# CHAPTER 6 - SPACE PLANNING AND CONCEPT LAYOUT

## 6.1 Pilot functional diagram (NOT TO SCALE)
ENTRANCE -> PUBLIC CAFÉ / ORDER COUNTER -> STAFF-VISIBLE ROOM CORRIDOR -> SOLO / DUO+ / GROUP

Separate wash/food handling from electrical/network cabinetry. Position shared washrooms, emergency exits and any accessible routes as required by the actual site and authority review. Put public charging points in a dry, visible location.

## 6.2 Growth layout rules
- Café should face the entry and be acoustically separated from the quiet room corridor.
- Corridors should avoid blind spots without putting cameras in private rooms.
- Use solid, safely installed partitions, sealed doors and a ventilation strategy. Acoustic foam alone is not sound isolation.
- Provide table space sufficient for notebooks, laptops and projects; avoid tiny coffee-shop round tables for work-focused users.
- Keep network cabinet, drink preparation, tool storage and rubbish handling separated.
- Plan airflow, cooling and heat loads per room; closed doors can make low-cost rooms stuffy very quickly.
- Do not invent legal exit widths, room dimensions or occupant load. Have the actual plan checked under applicable building, sanitary and fire rules.

## 6.3 Space envelope - design assumption only
Pilot space might be approximately 35-65 m2 if existing layout is suitable. The 14-room target plus café, access ways and services could require roughly 150-220 m2 or more depending on room sizes and code-driven arrangements. These are **not regulatory floor-area standards** or architectural measurements.

## 6.4 Acoustic acceptance test
From the neighboring room, play normal conversation and record whether words are intelligible. Check doors closed, HVAC running and electrical equipment on. Repeat after construction. The customer rule is **"Do not create noise that can be heard as a disturbance in the next room."** The business must offer sufficient actual isolation; it cannot fairly blame normal speaking customers for poor construction.

# CHAPTER 7 - PRIVACY, CAMERA CHOICE AND MINORS

## 7.1 Default surveillance boundary
No routine CCTV inside private rooms. Cameras, if installed, are limited to announced common areas such as entrance, corridor and cashier, positioned to minimize views into rooms. Post notices and document purpose, retention, access and incident handling in accordance with the National Privacy Commission's CCTV guidance [S1].

## 7.2 Optional portable room surveillance
**Not recommended for the pilot.** The originally proposed customer-requested portable camera creates serious consent, security, recording, child privacy and retention complexity. If revisited, require a written policy, a defensible legal basis, agreement of **all affected occupants**, additional safeguards for minors, restricted access and clear removal/retention rules; get a competent privacy/legal review before deployment. A requesting customer does not automatically authorize monitoring companions.

## 7.3 General safety
Provide a way to call the owner, visible emergency routes, reliable door releases, first-aid supplies, fire-safety equipment required by the authority, incident logs and safe maximum occupancies. Privacy **does not** mean staff are prohibited from responding to emergencies.

## 7.4 Student/minor protection
Check the applicable age and parental/guardian policy. No minors allowed to use heated, sharp, fume-generating or powered workshop tools unsupervised. Use only essential booking/guardian contact data; publish a privacy notice and deletion schedule. Do not require unnecessary student identification photocopies.

# CHAPTER 8 - EQUIPMENT, MATERIALS AND BORROWING

**Core kit:** desks, comfortable easy-clean chairs, task lighting, whiteboards, dry-erase markers, rulers, pencils, erasers, basic first-aid and appropriate waste bins.

**Shared creative kit:** cutting mats, scissors appropriate for the user's age, tape, glue sticks, paper, color markers, drafting supplies and hand tools chosen for low risk. Limit one kit per booking and count it back in.

**Premium kit (defer):** PCs, additional monitors, scanners, printers, specialist testing equipment, delicate electronic instruments.

**Hazardous workshop kit (separate approval):** drills, saws, soldering, hot glue, high-current bench power sources and volatile chemicals. Only introduce with an approved dedicated zone, instructions, PPE, ventilation, permits where needed and supervision.

Borrowing log fields: date/time, room, item/asset tag, condition at issue, borrower identifier, return time, returned condition, staff initials, damage note. Set clear normal-wear and accidental-damage policies; don't automatically accuse customers of theft.

# CHAPTER 9 - OWNER-OPERATED DAILY WORKFLOW

## 9.1 Before opening (20-40 minute checklist)
Inspect exits and extinguishers; check toilets/water/handwashing; visually check outlets; connect payment system; verify room controls and PisoWiFi; wipe tables; count drinks and supplies; update room availability. Record any unsafe equipment as OUT OF SERVICE.

## 9.2 During service
1. Greet and classify as café visitor or private-room booking.
2. Show prices and rules before payment.
3. Assign table boundary or room and log occupant count.
4. Issue proper invoice/document required by applicable tax rules.
5. Serve the food or drink; start café seating timer.
6. Explain add-ons; do not interrupt private-room users without reason.
7. Process extensions, booking changes and support requests.
8. Record each incident or outage and refund response.

## 9.3 Closing (20-45 minutes)
Clean surfaces; count room usage; count cash and coin boxes independently from electronic revenue; log wastage, supplies and broken tools; lock machines and high-value equipment; check network backups; close café stock and securely dispose of unsafe perishables.

## 9.4 Solo-operator capacity ceiling
Three rooms + 2-3 café tables can be plausible with packaged drinks/snacks, scheduled check-ins, few menu SKUs and streamlined POS. Fourteen private rooms plus café **is not a reasonable unattended or guaranteed one-person workload**. Recruit help when arrival queues, delayed cleaning, incidents or customer dissatisfaction rise. Provide a trusted relief operator for absence/emergencies.

These are **budget caps**, not supplier quotations. Any item over budget must trigger scope reduction; **never reduce fire safety, ventilation, sanitation or electrical protection** just to hit PHP 200k.

If there is no suitable owned/family space, if rent deposits are required, if major construction is needed, or if proper noise isolation is expensive, this budget will NOT cover the proposed pilot. Do not borrow large amounts solely to build a concept without proven paid demand.

## 10.2 What not to buy initially
No 14-room partition package; no full espresso machine/kitchen; no costly gaming PCs; no industrial tools; no full surveillance-in-rooms; no elaborate app development; no custom coin outlet at every table.

## 10.3 Phase gates
Spend only the next tranche when the previous stage is safe and justified:
- Gate A: land/site legally usable; written estimates; demand interviews.
- Gate B: permits/pathway understood; cash reserve protected.
- Gate C: limited build inspected and priced correctly.
- Gate D: soft opening with actual paid bookings and measured repeat demand.

# CHAPTER 11 - UNIT ECONOMICS AND BREAK-EVEN

## 11.1 Pilot room capacity
With 1 Solo (PHP 49/hr), 1 Duo+ (PHP 79/hr), 1 Group at four-person minimum (PHP 120/hr), the **combined maximum rate at minimum group charge is PHP 248/hour**.

Assuming 8 saleable hours/day and 26 days/month: 208 possible room-hours per room, **PHP 51,584 total monthly room revenue if all three sell every hour at those base rates**. This is *not* a demand prediction.

| Average utilization | Monthly room gross at posted rate |
|---|---:|
| 10% | PHP 5,158 |
| 20% | PHP 10,317 |
| 30% | PHP 15,475 |
| 50% | PHP 25,792 |
| 70% | PHP 36,109 |

Realized sales may be lower with four-hour discounts and cancellations, or higher if group rooms book five/six payers and add-ons sell. Treat such changes explicitly, not implicitly.

## 11.2 Example café contribution
If 12 transactions/day average PHP 65 and 26 opening days, monthly **café gross sales** = PHP 20,280. At a hypothetical **40% contribution margin after product-related variable costs**, contribution = PHP 8,112 before rent, labor, utilities, cleaning, fixed costs and owner pay.

Example at 20% rooms: PHP 10,317 room gross + PHP 8,112 café contribution + assumed PHP 1,500 net after variable costs from PisoWiFi/charging = about **PHP 19,929 available toward fixed costs and owner compensation**. This is not a forecast, and not necessarily profit.

## 11.3 The make-or-break distinction
Monthly **cash profit** = room sales + café sales + device/utility sales - ingredient/consumable costs - rent - electricity/internet - repairs - permits/tax cash flow - payroll/owner allowance - depreciation/replacement needs - other costs.

Distinguish **gross sales**, **gross margin/contribution**, **operating profit**, and **cash in bank**. Coins collected are sales, not automatically pure profit.

## 11.4 Conservative decision test
Before opening, model low / base / high bookings, exam season, ordinary classes, summer break and storms. Count the owner's working hours as a cost, even if you postpone paying yourself. Keep at least 3 months of realistic committed operating cash *after* opening costs if possible.

# CHAPTER 12 - OWNED PROPERTY, RENT OR LEASE?

A suitable owned/family property is generally better for an unproven owner-operated pilot because it removes monthly rent pressure. It is **not automatically legal for public commercial use**. Confirm zoning, owner permission, building use, commercial sanitation requirements and safety before inviting customers.

A lease may make sense later near a campus after recurring demand is proven. Model deposits, advance rent, fit-out that cannot be recovered, annual escalation, required restoration, noisy neighbors, landlord consent and permits.

Example only: PHP 20,000 monthly rent means PHP 60,000 rent over 3 months without any customers. A reserve of PHP 45,000 would not cover that rent alone. This is why a paid-lease first launch is rejected for the PHP 200,000 approach.

Do **not** assert that a rented business always closes within 3 months. Survival depends on costs, revenues and cash reserves, not a universal time limit.

# CHAPTER 13 - REGISTRATION, TAX AND COMPLIANCE

**This section is a preparation checklist, not legal advice. Confirm with the actual municipality/city, BIR Revenue District Office, DTI Negosyo Center, city health office and BFP.** Rules and thresholds can change.

## 13.1 Practical starting structure
Evaluate a **sole proprietorship** with declared activities covering café retail/food handling, study/workspace access, computer/equipment services, PisoWiFi and metered power where applicable. Use separate **internal revenue categories** in one accounting system. Different activities may still require distinct permits, inspections or tax handling.

## 13.2 Registration route
1. Ask local zoning/building authorities if the intended site can lawfully serve as a café and private study room operation.
2. DTI business name registration for a sole proprietorship, subject to correct classification [S2].
3. Apply for appropriate LGU permits and building/use clearances. Cagayan de Oro's city finance site lists business permit documentation and local process [S3]; other municipalities differ.
4. For food/drinks, ask the health office about the sanitary permit and any food-handler health certificate under PD 856 [S4].
5. Obtain applicable Fire Safety Inspection Certificate / BFP clearance and fix deficiencies [S5].
6. BIR business registration, invoice/recording setup, books and appropriate returns. The EOPT reform made **invoices** the primary evidence for both goods and service sales, subject to detailed rules [S6].
7. Investigate Barangay Micro Business Enterprise (BMBE) eligibility, rather than claiming it by default. RA 9178's broad definition covers certain small trading/service businesses with total assets up to PHP 3m, excluding qualifying land [S7]. Get current eligibility and tax-treatment confirmation for the *actual* mix of activities.

## 13.3 Tax truthfulness
Legal ways to avoid overpayment: accurate books, properly supported business expenses under the chosen regime, timely filings, correct registration, and confirmed eligibility for any incentive. Don't underdeclare coin collections, relabel sales as donations, or pretend the café and private rooms are an unregistered private club.

The **8% income tax option** may be available for certain non-VAT taxpayers under specific requirements; mixed-income workers have different treatment for the PHP 250,000 reduction. See BIR RR 8-2018 [S8], and confirm the current rule with your RDO. **A qualified income-tax-exempt BMBE cannot simply stack its exemption and the 8% business option.** BMBE is not a universal exemption from every tax.

Do not infer tax liability from diagram, room count or brand name. Turnover, income category, legal status and chosen tax regime matter. File even where a particular tax due is zero if required.

## 13.4 Retained source documents
Keep DTI/permit certificates, approved invoices, order logs, cancellation/refund trail, Z-read or daily POS totals, coin collections reconciliations, payment proofs, stock counts, equipment capital register and supplier invoices.

# CHAPTER 14 - LAUNCH MARKETING AND CUSTOMER RESEARCH

## 14.1 Demand validation before paying for walls
Interview at least 30-50 prospective customers across local student segments; observe peak walking traffic and schedules; collect bookings or refundable reservations if lawful and operationally ready. Ask about actual room duration, comfortable price, two-hour café use, pain points and preferred location. A survey "yes" is less convincing than paying for a booked hour.

## 14.2 Proposed offer
"Study quietly. Create freely. Respect the next room." Show pictures of the actual rooms and the true power/Wi-Fi policy. Explicitly state rates per *room* versus per *head*. Don't suggest the café includes free Wi-Fi.

## 14.3 Channels
Community student groups with permission, referrals, campus-adjacent flyers where allowed, local maps/business profile, student research demo days, and appointment-based soft opening. Track which sources create real paid bookings.

## 14.4 Fair incentives
Use first-booking trials or off-peak bundles instead of indefinite low rates. Avoid discounting peak group-project demand below cost. Do not create a loyalty program until the baseline checkout is reliable.

# CHAPTER 15 - FIRST 90 DAYS: EXECUTION PLAN

## Days 1-15: validate and approve
- Determine exact property and ownership/lease permission.
- Confirm location's legal commercial use with appropriate authorities.
- Gather at least 3 fit-out and electrical quotations and 2-3 ventilation options.
- Survey students and ask for real paid-reservation intent.
- Obtain permit, fire, food-handling and privacy guidance; do not open prematurely.
- Reject the concept or reduce scope if the sound/noise requirement cannot be achieved affordably.

## Days 16-30: build the pilot
- Convert just one solo, one Duo+ and one group room.
- Install safe tables, outlets, ventilation, signage and network segregation.
- Establish cashier, timer, stock, tool-issue log and complaint process.
- Test shared corridor cameras only; no internal-room CCTV.
- Test emergency access, lights, room occupancy and acoustic leakage.

## Days 31-60: controlled soft opening
- Open predictable owner-attended hours.
- Sell the advertised service, issue proper documents and record true money flows.
- Measure every paid room-hour, café order, gross margin, utility expense and complaint.
- Gather anonymous satisfaction results but don't retain unnecessary student data.
- Adjust only one major assumption at a time.

## Days 61-90: expand, revise or pause
- Calculate occupancy by room type, paid repeat visits, café contribution and net cash.
- Expand most demanded room type only if finances and layout support it.
- If occupancy is weak, investigate address visibility, peak hours, product fit and price before spending on new rooms.
- Pause any expansion if operating cash reserve is being consumed too quickly.

# CHAPTER 16 - OPERATIONS SCORECARD AND DECISION GATES

## 16.1 Weekly KPI log
| Metric | How calculated | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Paid occupancy | Paid room-hours / available room-hours | Core demand |
| Revenue / available room-hour | Room revenue / available room-hours | Price plus demand |
| Repeat booking share | Repeat booked parties / all parties | Product usefulness |
| Café average ticket | Café sales / café transactions | Unit economics |
| Café gross margin | (Café sales - direct goods cost)/sales | Real contribution |
| Add-on take rate | Buyers of extras / total eligible customers | Value of extras |
| Customer complaints | Count and severity; root causes | Service quality |
| Cash runway | Cash reserve / net monthly burn | Survival |

## 16.2 Go / hold / stop criteria
**GO** when the space is legally and physically safe, the reserve remains protected, and sustained paid demand plus positive unit margins are visible.

**HOLD** when customers like the concept but occupancy is inconsistent, sound isolation needs correction, or owner workload rises above safe capacity.

**STOP/REDESIGN** when the building is not approvable, hidden electrical/fire risks remain, repeated incidents are unresolved, or continued operation drains protected household cash.

# APPENDIX A - PUBLIC HOUSE RULES (POSTER COPY)

**PRIVATE ROOMS**
- Keep noise low enough that neighboring rooms are not disturbed.
- Only registered occupants may enter; obey Solo 1 / Duo+ 3 / Group 6 maximum.
- No room cameras by default. Hallway security systems may operate with posted notices.
- Respect equipment, Wi-Fi policies, and booking times.
- Do not use hazardous or noisy tools in ordinary study rooms.

**PUBLIC CAFÉ**
- 1-2 guests: each orders anything; first 2 hours of seating included.
- Parties of 3+: PHP 60 minimum per person upfront, fully usable for food/drinks.
- Extra time: trial PHP 20/head/hour if seats are available.
- Each table has its own virtual personal zone, including floor space for belongings.
- No activities spread across the floor, neighboring zones, aisles or emergency paths.
- Please keep activities safe, tidy and considerate.
- Café internet is via paid PisoWiFi; café charging stations are paid separately.

# FINAL BUSINESS DECISION

**Build the minimum safe, legally permitted version, not the dream capacity.** Keep the original mature vision of **8 Solo + 4 Duo+ + 2 Group** as the scalable target. Treat the small café as a customer-acquisition and complementary-sales channel rather than relying on it to subsidize unlimited free seating.

**Launch condition:** legally permitted suitable property, safe outlets/ventilation, workable sound separation, actual student interest, transparent pricing, protected cash reserve, and a manageable owner-attended operating schedule.

**Success condition:** repeat paid bookings, clear weekly cash reporting and a growing reserve justify the next room. Expand one room at a time rather than committing to an expensive unfamiliar rental property.
